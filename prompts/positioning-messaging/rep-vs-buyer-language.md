# Rep vs. buyer language

**What this does**: Shows where your reps and your buyers describe the same thing in different words — so you can either coach the reps or borrow the buyers' language.

**When to use it**: You suspect there's a translation gap on calls. Reps pitch "platform capabilities"; buyers say "I just need to get the report out on Friday." You want to see the gap.

## Paste this into Claude

```
On our sales calls, pull the language reps use to describe what we do vs. the language buyers use. Group by topic — for each topic, show 2-3 verbatim quotes from reps and 2-3 verbatim quotes from buyers. Highlight any topic where the two sides are clearly talking past each other. Focus on the last 90 days.
```

## What you'll see back

- A side-by-side per topic: rep language vs. buyer language.
- Verbatim quotes from each side, with speaker roles.
- A "talking past each other" callout where the gap is largest.
- A short recommendation: which language wins, and why.

## Variations

- One rep: `Only calls from {rep name}.`
- One product line: `Only calls discussing {product or feature}.`
- Add: `End with a coaching note for reps.`

## Tips

- Buyer language almost always wins. The exception is when reps are pulling buyers up the abstraction ladder on purpose.
- If a rep's language is suspiciously similar to the buyers' — congratulations, they've been listening. That's the model.
- Pair with [stress-test a message](stress-test-a-message.md) for new copy.
