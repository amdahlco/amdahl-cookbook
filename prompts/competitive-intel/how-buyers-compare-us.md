# How buyers compare us

**What this does**: Pulls the actual trade-offs buyers articulate when they're choosing between you and a specific competitor.

**When to use it**: You're working on positioning, sales enablement, or a battle card and you want the comparison in *buyer* words, not marketing words.

## Paste this into Claude

```
On calls where buyers compared us to {competitor name}, what trade-offs did they actually articulate? Give me the verbatim language they used for: where they thought we were stronger, where they thought the competitor was stronger, and where they thought it was a tie. Group by theme. Include speaker role and rough date for each quote.
```

## What you'll see back

- Three buckets: we win / they win / tie, with quotes in each.
- Themes grouped (e.g. "implementation speed", "reporting depth", "price").
- Speaker role on each quote (champion, exec, end user) — the role matters.
- A "what to lean into / what to neutralize" summary.

## Variations

- Segment: `Only among enterprise buyers.`
- Stage: `Only quotes from late-stage deals (post-demo).`
- Add: `End with 3 reframes for the places they think the competitor wins.`

## Tips

- The "tie" bucket is the most useful — those are the features you're spending money on that nobody cares about.
- Watch for the same theme appearing in *both* "we win" and "they win" — that's a sign of segment differences.
- Roles matter: end users say different things than economic buyers.
