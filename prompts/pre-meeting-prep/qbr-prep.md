# QBR prep

**What this does**: Builds the prep doc for a customer QBR — last-quarter recap with usage and verbatim moments, risk list with named signals, expansion angles grounded in what the customer actually said they care about, a recommended agenda, and 3 customer quotes you should be ready to reference in the room.

**When to use it**: "I have a QBR with {customer} in a few days. I want to walk in knowing the wins to celebrate, the risks to flag, and the next thing worth selling — all grounded in their year, not a generic template."

## Why this matters

QBRs go wrong two ways. The first is the **template QBR** — a deck where 80% of the slides are the same every quarter for every customer, and the customer can tell. The second is the **vanity QBR** — a deck full of usage charts pointing up and to the right that ignores the three things the customer has been quietly frustrated about for two months. Both end the same way: the customer nods politely, the renewal conversation gets harder, and the expansion never happens.

The good QBR is built around three honest sections: (1) what actually happened last quarter from their side, in their language; (2) the risks you're going to flag *first*, before they raise them; and (3) the expansion angle that's grounded in something they themselves said. The last one is the hardest — and the one that turns the QBR from a status meeting into a buying conversation.

## Paste this into Claude

```
I have a QBR with {company name or domain} on {date}. Attendees on their side: {names + roles, if known}. Specific business unit or product line in scope: {if applicable; otherwise say "all"}.

Build me a QBR prep doc.

Wave 1 (run these in parallel):
- Quarter recap: what they used in the last quarter (products, features, volume / scale), what worked, what didn't, what was launched or rolled out from our side, any escalations or support touches. Pull 3–5 verbatim quotes from calls during the quarter that capture value moments OR friction moments.
- Risk surface: sentiment shifts across the quarter (warmer / colder), any unanswered objections or unresolved issues, any contract or commercial concerns raised, any champion changes (gained, lost, gone quiet), any competitive mentions.
- Expansion signal: in the last 12 months on calls, what have they said they care about that we DON'T currently sell or fully cover? Verbatim quotes. Cross-reference against our product / pricing surface to identify the 1–2 cleanest expansion angles.
- Attendee context: for each named attendee, what they've said this year (verbatim) and what they specifically seem to care about.

Wave 2 (after wave 1):
Produce a QBR prep doc with this structure:
1. Quarter snapshot (3 lines) — overall posture, biggest win, biggest risk.
2. Recap section — usage signal + 2 value moments (with quotes) + 1–2 friction moments (with quotes).
3. Risk section — 3 ranked risks. For each: the signal, the impact, the action.
4. Expansion angles — 1–2 angles, each grounded in a verbatim customer quote.
5. Attendee guide — what each attendee likely cares most about.
6. Recommended agenda with rough timing (60-min default; adjust if I tell you otherwise).
7. The 3 quotes from the quarter I should be ready to reference verbatim in the room.

Cite call dates on every quote. No fabrication — if a section is thin, say so.
```

## What you'll see back

- A quarter-recap section with usage signal and named value + friction moments.
- A ranked risk list with named signals — not "we should be careful," but "the champion has stopped attending the bi-weekly since {date} — the signal."
- 1–2 expansion angles, each grounded in a customer quote (not "they might want X" — "they said X verbatim on Y date").
- An attendee guide so you know who cares about what.
- A recommended agenda you can lift into the deck outline.
- 3 quotes to reference verbatim — the most useful asset in the room.

## How to actually use it

1. **Run it 3–5 days before the QBR.** You want enough time to do follow-up research on the expansion angle before you walk in.
2. **Read the risk section first, and resolve as much as you can before the QBR.** A risk you've already addressed in a 1:1 is one you don't have to handle in front of the room.
3. **The expansion angles are the ROI of the QBR.** Don't bury them in slide 18 — build them into the agenda explicitly, grounded in their own quote.
4. **Walk into the room with the 3 reference quotes memorized.** Quoting a customer back to themselves — using their own words from a call 4 months ago — is the single highest-trust move available in a QBR.

## Variations

- **Multi-stakeholder QBR**: "Add a per-attendee deep-dive — what each one has said this year and how they likely feel going in."
- **Renewal-adjacent QBR**: "Flag anything that affects the renewal conversation — sentiment, champion changes, competitive mentions, contract concerns." (Or run [renewal prep](renewal-prep.md) instead.)
- **Multi-product customer**: "Break the recap, risks, and expansion sections out by product line."
- **Opening slide**: "Draft the 5-bullet opening recap slide that summarizes the quarter from their perspective."

## Tips

- **Expansion angles that aren't grounded in a quote are guesses.** If the angle has no verbatim quote behind it, demote it or kill it.
- **Champion changes are the single highest-impact risk signal.** Don't skip that subsection.
- **The "what they said" section is what flips the QBR from "your update" to "their narrative."** Quote them back to themselves.
- **Pair with [renewal prep](renewal-prep.md)** when the renewal is within 90 days of the QBR — the prep overlaps significantly.
- **Re-run the day after the QBR** with "What new signal did this QBR surface, and what does it change about the account plan?" — turns the meeting into the next account move.
