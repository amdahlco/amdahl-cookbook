# What separates won from lost

**What this does**: Surfaces the specific differences between deals that closed and deals that didn't — in the same segment, in the same window.

**When to use it**: You want to know what good actually looks like. Not "what did we do in the wins" — what did we do in the wins that we *didn't* do in the losses.

## Paste this into Claude

```
Compare our closed-won deals against our closed-lost deals in {segment or time window — e.g. "the last 12 months among enterprise prospects"}. Don't just describe each side — tell me what's actually different. What did wins have that losses didn't? Look at: stakeholders engaged, meetings held, objections raised and resolved, language buyers used, competitive presence, time-to-value mentioned, executive sponsorship. End with the 3 sharpest discriminators — the things most likely to predict win vs. lose.
```

## What you'll see back

- A diff, not two profiles — what's *different* between wins and losses.
- Discriminators across: stakeholders, objections handled, language, competitive context.
- 3 ranked predictors at the end.
- Often: a couple of surprising findings worth pulling into a sales meeting.

## Variations

- Segment-narrow: `Just SMB.`
- ICP refinement: `Translate the discriminators into ICP criteria.`
- Add: `Suggest 2 changes to our discovery checklist based on this.`

## Tips

- This is the recipe to run before any sales-motion rework.
- Watch for time-to-value language — wins almost always have a specific timeline; losses are vague.
- The "stakeholders engaged" diff is usually the single biggest predictor. Don't skip it.
